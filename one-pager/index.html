Research concept · Context v1 "Alder"

A flight simulator for AI-assisted development.

Using an AI coding assistant well is a skill of judgment, not typing. You build the reflexes the way pilots do — in a simulator where the traps are deliberate, the stakes are zero, and a hidden instrument tells you whether you actually flew it right.

A purpose-built kata reveals where AI slips and where random prompting provably can't reach the goal — but the kata is only one instance. The product is the Context that generates more, in any domain and any stack.

The gap nobody fills

Three bands exist; the useful middle is empty.

Assessment — crowdedGrades you once, to hire. CodeSignal, HackerEarth, Formation; Meta & Google now score "how you drive AI." Proctored, one-shot.
Content — saturatedTells you the traps. "8 AI coding traps" listicles, essays on plausible-but-wrong code. Reading one never builds the reflex.
Practice — openLets you hit the traps yourself, repeatedly, self-serve, on a seeded codebase with a grader that scores the outcome and the driving. This.

Five phases, on a clock

The domain and stack change; the flow never does. Each phase is one discipline under pressure.

01

Understand

Map boundaries and invariants before anything moves. Read, don't delegate.

02

Fix

A symptom, not a line number. Reproduce with a failing test first.

03

Feature

Underspecified on purpose. Gather requirements; dodge the trap.

04

Improve

Find the ranked latent defects — from the code, not from memory.

05

Review

Name your own weakness, the trade-off, and what you'd revisit.

One interface, three modes

The harness runs a practice for you on a throwaway clone — never the original — with one agent executing your prompts and a separate examiner grading how you drove, from its own golden context.

readLearn the Context before you try it — its disciplines, best practices, and failure modes.
assessDrive it solo. The assistant runs your prompts; no live help. A written debrief scores each round at the end.
trainDrive it coached. The harness catches a weak prompt before its consequence compounds.

suggested path:  read → assess → train  ·  goal: the Context's best practices become reflexes

The instrument that doesn't lie

The seed kata: a subscription-box scheduler whose cutoff ignores the customer's timezone. 15 unit tests stay green — the bug is invisible to them. The hidden grader runs under three server timezones and scores the worst case. A fix that only works on your machine doesn't count.

Why a hidden oracle

Without a check it can run, "looks done" is the only signal — and a green suite is exactly what automation bias trusts. The oracle is the objective gate.

  • node --test → 15 / 15 green, bug still live
  • npm run grade → worst-case 0 / 7
  • timezone-aware fix → 7 / 7 everywhere

How you drove — reproduced before fixing, rejected the over-build, matched the altitude — is the other half, and the half this trains.
